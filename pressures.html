Page 2 · Student wellbeing in India

Seven pressures.
Small next steps.

These situations are common, but each student’s experience is their own. Choose one manageable step; you do not have to fix everything today.

A visual pause

Every student carries it differently.

These classroom scenes are illustrative, not evidence about any one student or country.

01

Marks, rank & board results

The moment: Family messages ask for your percentage before you have had time to process the result.

Try: Tell one trusted person how you feel. Prepare a simple reply for relatives: “I’m still processing it; I’ll talk when I’m ready.” A score is information, not your worth.

02

JEE / NEET preparation

The moment: A difficult mock test makes the entire evening feel like a prediction of your future.

Try: Review three errors you can learn from and make a small plan for the next study session. Treat a mock as practice data, not a verdict. Protect sleep and ask a teacher for help with a topic.

03

“Log kya kahenge”

The moment: At a family gathering, a relative compares your college or marks with someone else’s.

Try: Keep one calm sentence ready: “I’m working on my own plan.” Mute comparison-heavy feeds for a while and speak to a friend who lets you be yourself.

04

Coaching & living away from home

The moment: Long coaching days end in a rented room where nobody notices how quiet you have become.

Try: Put one regular call home or meal with a classmate on your calendar. Step outside for a short break and talk to a trusted teacher, hostel warden or counsellor if loneliness continues.

05

Placements & job anxiety

The moment: A placement list goes up and it feels as if everyone can see who has not received an offer.

Try: Ask your placement office for concrete next steps. Improve one project or application at a time; a rejection is not a measure of your value.

06

Fees, loans & family expectations

The moment: Fee reminders arrive while you are also trying to meet family expectations and keep up with classes.

Try: Write down the actual deadlines and amounts, then ask your college office about scholarships, waivers or payment options. Share the plan with someone you trust.

07

Sleep & phone habits

The moment: “One more scroll” pushes bedtime later, even when tomorrow starts early.

Try: Charge the phone away from your bed and choose a gentle wind-down reminder. If sleep or scrolling feels difficult to change, ask a counsellor or health professional for support.
